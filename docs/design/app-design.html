AMN Drift

A living sky behind every screen. Soft layers of the widget palettes drift, fade and trade places on their own clocks, so the background never settles into a loop. Frosted glass with moving grain sits on top.

"Real" is the proposed speed: a layer takes 17–29 seconds to fade into its next palette. 20× shows the drift quickly. With reduce motion on (or the phone's own setting), the sky holds still on one palette and changes only when the app opens.

How the drift works

Four layers, each on its own clock, each picking its next palette from all 28 widget skies (7 conditions × dawn, day, dusk, night) in the phone's light or dark version.

Why it never feels like a loop

  • Different clocks: 37, 29, 23 and 17 seconds share no common beat, so the same combination doesn't come back for hours.
  • Random walk, not a playlist: each layer picks its next palette by a seeded hash, never the one it's on and never the one another layer just took.
  • Orbits with odd ratios: each glow follows two overlapping sine paths whose periods don't divide evenly, so the paths never retrace.
  • Continuous fades: a layer is always halfway between two palettes; nothing ever snaps.

Surfaces

Content sits on frosted glass: a strong blur, a light or dark tint, a hairline edge with a highlight on top, and a grain texture that shifts a few times a second. The accent (buttons, toggles, chevrons) is taken from the sky and adjusted until it reads on the glass.

Accent from the sky

  • Take the near glow's current color, keep its hue, raise saturation to at least 55%.
  • Set lightness so it reaches 4.5:1 against the glass: about 40% on light glass, 72% on dark.
  • Ease changes over 2 seconds, so it drifts with the sky instead of jumping.
  • Text on the accent (button labels) is near-black or near-white, whichever contrasts more.

Building it in Flutter

Background

  • One fragment shader (.frag, Flutter's FragmentProgram) draws the base gradient and the three glows in a single pass on the GPU. The palettes and positions are passed in as uniforms each frame from Dart.
  • Tilt: sensors_plus gyroscope, low-pass filtered, mapped to ±24 dp per depth unit.
  • Pauses when the app is in the background or the screen is off, and runs at 30 fps, which is plenty for slow drift.
  • Reduce motion (the system "Remove animations" setting): a still sky on one palette, chosen fresh each time the app opens.

Glass and grain

  • Glass: BackdropFilter with a 22 dp blur per card, clipped to its rounded shape. Two or three cards per screen keep the cost low.
  • Grain: one small pre-made noise tile, drawn over each card with overlay blending, its offset jumping about 6 times a second. No per-frame noise is generated, so it's nearly free.
  • Check on the A55: if the blur ever drops frames, the fallback is the same glass without blur (tint and grain only).

Structure

  • A new packages/amn_ui with the theme (colors, type, radii), DriftBackground, GlassCard, the accent notifier and the shader. Tools and the widget pages use it.
  • The palettes come from one shared table, so the widgets and the app always use the same skies.
