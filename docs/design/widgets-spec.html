AMN Widgets

The weather and clock widgets for AMN, my personal toolbox app. They are built for my Samsung phone; the code is public, with no ads. Colors follow the sky, numbers are big and condensed, and a single line tells you what changed. Use the controls to step through conditions, times of day and settings. The full build spec is further down.

Decisions

Two changes, both decided and built. The mockups follow the controls at the top of the page: condition, time of day, theme, units and 12/24h.

1 · Refresh button on the weather widget · decided

A small round button placed where each size has free space. The art owns the top-right corner, so:

  • 4×2: right after the time in the header.
  • 4×3 and 4×4: merged with the "Updated" label into one chip, top right.
  • 2×2: bottom right, next to the summary chip.
  • 2×1: no button, since there's no room. Tapping opens the edit page, which has Refresh now.
  • Tap: refreshes this widget's location and forecast without opening the app. Tapping anywhere else still opens the edit page. The icon is 16 dp, but its tap area is padded to about 40 dp.
  • States: widgets can't animate, so a tap switches the button to a dimmed "Updating…" until the new forecast arrives. With no connection it shows "Offline" for a few seconds, then goes back.
  • Just now: for two minutes after any update, whether from a tap or the 30-minute refresh, the button shows a dimmed "Just now" and taps do nothing. Then it goes back to "x min ago". At 4×2 the button only gets words while updating or just updated. At 2×2 it stays an icon and dims.
  • Cost: at most one Open-Meteo request per tap, and none within two minutes of the last update.
  • 2×2 summaries are capped at 16 characters so the chip and the button share the bottom row.

2 · Clock with weather art · decided: B

Today the clock's art is the sun or moon for the city's time of day. Here it's replaced by the city's weather. Three cities, each in three versions:

  • A · Weather art, time-of-day colors: the art tells the weather and the colors still tell the time, so a cloudy afternoon and a cloudy night look different. Clear and partly cloudy switch to their moon versions at night. The small weather icon in the text line is dropped, since the art already shows it. The line keeps just the temperature ("Night · 26°"). The art is drawn at 80%, because clouds and rain are taller than a plain sun and would otherwise reach the time.
  • B · Weather art, weather colors: matches the weather widget exactly. That ties the two together, but the time of day then only shows in the "Night"/"Day" label. Next to a weather widget for the same city, it reads as a duplicate.
  • What's lost in both: the sun on the horizon at dawn and dusk. It can stay for clear skies, as in A here.

Decided: B. The clock takes the weather widget's look for its city: condition art and condition colors, with "Night"/"Day" in the text line. With weather switched off, or before the first fetch, it falls back to the sun and moon on time-of-day colors. Where the clock has room beside or above the time, the art is drawn larger and runs just slightly (12%) off the top-right corner, so the widget doesn't look empty. At the tight 2×1 it stays smaller and runs further off. The playground clocks above now use B.

Brief

Home
Part of AMN, a personal toolbox app. Built for one Samsung phone running One UI on Android 14 or later. The code is public on GitHub, but there is no compatibility work for other phones, older Android versions or F-Droid.
Architecture
A Flutter app (dev.amaan.amn) for the shell, the in-app tools and the edit pages. The widgets, weather fetching and all the rules are native Kotlin inside android/. Android widgets only for now.
Look
Expressive and bold. Soft 3D illustrations on saturated sky gradients. Both widgets share type, shapes and color logic.
Color
Weather: driven by the current condition and time of day. Clock: driven by the time of day in the chosen city. Both get darker versions when the phone is in dark mode.
Type
Condensed display numerals (Barlow Condensed Bold, OFL). Labels in the system sans.
Weather content
City, current time, temperature, condition, summary line, high/low, feels-like, chance and amount of precipitation, hourly strip, 3-day outlook.
Clock content
Digital time for one chosen city, city name, time difference from you, day/night, and the city's weather (art, colors and temperature, refreshed hourly). 12/24h, seconds and weather are settings.
Data
Open-Meteo, refreshed about every 30 minutes. Units are set separately for temperature and precipitation.
Tap
Opens that widget's edit page. AMN has no other screens for now.

Color

Each widget state has one background gradient (top-left to bottom-right) and one text color. The weather widget uses its condition's row. The clock uses the Clear row, so the two widgets match when the sky is clear. Every number below is the worst-case contrast of the text against either end of the gradient, computed live from the same rule the app will use.

  • Time of day. Dawn runs from 30 min before to 60 min after sunrise, dusk from 60 min before to 30 min after sunset. Night is after dusk until dawn. (The mockup uses fixed hours: dawn 06–08, day 08–17, dusk 17–19.)
  • Dawn and dusk. Clear has hand-picked dawn and dusk gradients. Other conditions mix their day gradient toward the Clear dawn/dusk colors (38% dawn, 35–45% dusk).
  • Dark mode. Night gradients stay as they are. Dawn, day and dusk gradients mix toward #0A0C15 (55% top, 62% bottom).
  • Text color. Use the state's preferred text color if it reaches 4.5:1 against both ends of the gradient. Otherwise pick the light or dark text color that scores higher. If that still misses 4.5:1, push both gradient stops 8% away from the text color, and repeat until it passes.
  • Secondary text keeps the same color at a lighter weight. Never use transparency for text. Chips and the hourly panel fill with the text color at 15% and 11%.

Summary line

One short line, picked in two tiers. Urgent rules change what you do, so they're checked in order and the first that applies wins. Otherwise every "flavor" rule that applies becomes a candidate, and a seed rotates between candidates and their phrasings. The widget changes the seed every two hours (offset per widget), so the line varies through the day without flickering between redraws. Each size only uses phrasings that fit: 17 characters at 2×1, 16 at 2×2 (it shares its bottom row with the refresh button), 22 at 4×2 and 24 at 4×3 and 4×4. Temperatures in the text follow the unit setting. The widget lab's Summary engine panel shows every rule's verdict and the arithmetic for any condition, hour and seed.

  • Precipitation amount: rain shows precipitation_sum in mm or in. Snow shows snowfall_sum in cm or in.
  • GPS location: the platform LocationManager.getCurrentLocation with coarse permission. That is enough for weather and keeps the public repo free of Play Services setup.
  • City name for GPS: Open-Meteo has no reverse geocoding. Android's Geocoder works on the Samsung phone (Android 13+ has an async version). If it ever returns nothing, show "Current location".
  • Pinned city search: Open-Meteo Geocoding API. The same search finds the clock's city, and the app stores its IANA time zone and coordinates.

Behavior

  • Refresh: WorkManager runs every 30 minutes when a network is available. It also refreshes when the app opens and when GPS location moves more than 5 km.
  • "Updated" label: shown on 4×3 and 4×4 ("Updated 12 min ago"). It is recalculated at each widget update, so it moves in refresh-sized steps.
  • Stale data: after 3 hours, the label reads "Updated 3 h ago" with a warning icon. Sizes without the label show the icon next to the time. The widget always shows the last good data and never goes blank offline.
  • Time: both widgets show time with a TextClock and a fixed time zone, so it stays exact without widget updates.
  • Clock updates are needed only when the time-of-day phase changes, when midnight passes in either zone ("tomorrow" label), and when the phone's time zone changes (ACTION_TIMEZONE_CHANGED). Schedule the next one with WorkManager or an inexact alarm. No per-minute updates are needed.
  • Tap: opens that widget's edit page in its Editing state. Once AMN has a weather screen, tapping the weather widget can go there instead, and the edit page stays one long-press away.
  • Setup: weather asks for "Current location" or a city search when first added. The clock asks for a city, 12/24h and seconds. Both can be changed later from the widget's long-press Settings option on One UI (widgetFeatures="reconfigurable") or inside AMN.
  • Battery: One UI may put AMN to sleep and delay refreshes. Add AMN to "Never sleeping apps" in the phone's battery settings, and show a hint on the weather screen if refreshes fall more than an hour behind.
  • Several clocks: each clock widget instance stores its own city, so you can place a few side by side.

  • Wiring: WidgetConfigActivity is a small FlutterActivity subclass, registered as android:configure in both widget info XMLs with widgetFeatures="reconfigurable". It reads EXTRA_APPWIDGET_ID, sets RESULT_CANCELED straight away, and opens the route for that widget kind with the id and an add/edit flag. The widget's tap action starts the same activity in edit mode.
  • Preview: the real widget, shown in a platform view (AndroidView, view type amn/widget-preview). Kotlin runs GlanceAppWidget.compose(context, size, state) with the unsaved settings, then RemoteViews.apply(), and puts the result in a FrameLayout. Every settings change on the page re-renders it. Wrap it in IgnorePointer so the widget's own tap action doesn't fire, and clip the corners in Flutter, since One UI only does that on the home screen. If the platform view misbehaves on One UI, fall back to drawing the preview in Flutter from values Kotlin computes.
  • Preview data: weather uses the last cached forecast. On the very first add, before the first fetch finishes, it shows sample data with a "Loading weather…" note.
  • Storage: Kotlin owns the settings. Per-widget settings live in Glance state, keyed by the widget id. Weather: location mode and pinned city (name, latitude, longitude). Clock: city name, IANA time zone, latitude and longitude, 12/24h and seconds. Units are app-wide in DataStore and shared by all weather widgets. Flutter reads and writes all of them only through the channel.
  • City search: done in Flutter (http package) against the Open-Meteo Geocoding API. It starts at 2 characters, waits 300 ms after typing stops, and shows up to 5 results with name, region and country. Clock results show each city's current time, using the time zone the API returns. With no match: "No city matches '…'. Check the spelling, or try a larger city nearby." When offline: "You're offline. Search needs a connection. Your current city stays."
  • Location permission: requested from Flutter with permission_handler. It asks for approximate location first, then explains why "Allow all the time" is needed (refreshes run in the background) and opens settings. If either is refused, the card says "Location is off for AMN" with an Allow button. Choose a city stays available, and the widget uses the last pinned city.
  • Defaults when adding: weather starts on Current location if permission is already granted. Otherwise it starts on Choose a city with the search field focused. The clock starts with the phone's 12/24h setting and seconds off. Add to home screen stays disabled until a city is picked.
  • Cold start: opening the page from a widget starts the Flutter engine, which takes about half a second. Give the activity a launch theme in the widget's colors so the wait doesn't flash white.
  • Look: follows the phone's light or dark theme, with AMN's orange as the accent. The title uses Barlow Condensed (bundled in Flutter assets/fonts too). Everything else uses the system font.

Architecture

Kotlin computes, Flutter displays. Widgets refresh while AMN is closed, so everything a widget needs runs natively without starting Flutter. Flutter owns every screen.

Building in Glance

Correction to what I said earlier: custom fonts don't have to become bitmaps. Glance's Text only takes system fonts, but Glance can embed a classic XML layout through AndroidRemoteViews. A TextClock or TextView in that layout can use android:fontFamily="@font/barlow_condensed_bold" (Android 8+). That keeps the clock exact, keeps seconds possible, and avoids redrawing every minute. Prototype it first on the Samsung phone. If the font loads there, the fallbacks below aren't needed.

Accessibility

  • Contrast: all text reaches 4.5:1 against both ends of its background (see the Color section).
  • TalkBack: give each widget one contentDescription that reads as a sentence. The examples under the mockups update with the controls.
  • Font scaling: labels in sp with maxLines = 1 and an ellipsis. Numerals in dp, because they define the layout. Test at font scale 1.3 and 2.0; Android 14's nonlinear scaling grows large text less.
  • Meaning without color: conditions always have art and, at 4×3 and up, a written name. Day/night on the clock has a sun or moon and, at 2×2, a word.
